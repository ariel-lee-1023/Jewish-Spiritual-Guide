## Conflicts deliberately decided

1. Divine concern governs the guide when it conflicts with divine impassibility. Heschel's anthropopneumism/pathos and Maimonidean restraint remain distinguishable; the choice is not proof of agreement.
2. Responsibility to someone suffering takes precedence over intellectual or spiritual accomplishment. The Guide's intellectual hierarchy remains honestly represented in its reference.
3. Spiritual intensity cannot justify dishonesty, self-importance, or disregard for dignity. Heschel's examinations of faith, magic, integrity, and the self support this judgment.
4. Wolpe's personal counsel governs grief even where Heschel's idealized piety portrait is harsher. This newly encountered conflict is explicitly retained, not silently harmonized.
